## 9. Where Forge must not compete (yet)

1. **Exercise library breadth.** 63 curated movements beat 5,600 shallow entries for demo clarity, but production needs 500–1,000 high-quality movements, not 10,000 scraped ones.
2. **Food database breadth.** Do not fight MFP on packaged-food SKUs. Win on photo-first home food and regional coverage, then integrate Open Food Facts/barcode.
3. **Social network scale.** Do not try to be Strava. Share out to Strava/Instagram; keep in-app social lightweight and training-relevant.
4. **Hardware.** No wearable. Ingest Apple Health/Health Connect and partner APIs.
5. **Gym ERP.** No memberships, access control or staff scheduling. Integrate with GYM-One/GymFlow-style systems later if needed.

## 11. Bottom line

- **Hevy/Strong** own fast logging; **Fitbod** owns generation; **Boostcamp** owns program credibility; **MFP** owns food breadth; **Strava** owns social identity; **wger/openGym** own self-host privacy.
- **Nobody owns the loop.** Forge's right to win is the loop: sense → decide → adapt → prove it worked.
- The demo proves the loop is buildable and delightful. The production roadmap must now prove it is reliable, native, and defensible.
